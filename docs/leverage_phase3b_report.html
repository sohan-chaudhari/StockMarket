§2 The unit-conversion decision — reported before implementing, as instructed

The engine has exactly two cutoff units: trading sessions, or calendar days. There is no native "weeks" or "months" unit.

RetentionRule has after_days and after_trading_sessions — nothing else. "2 years," "5 years," and "10 years" are calendar-time concepts that don't map onto either field without a conversion. I did not invent a new engine concept to avoid this — I used the existing after_trading_sessions field (matching your explicit preference for trading-session logic over naive calendar-day math, and matching the convention every other rule in this file already uses) and converted years → sessions using an explicit, documented constant: NSE_SESSIONS_PER_YEAR = 250, defined once in config/retention_policy.py and referenced by both the 1D and 1W rules.

250 is the commonly-cited approximate NSE trading-session count per calendar year — actual years vary roughly 245–252 depending on that year's holiday count. This is a real approximation, not a measured value, and it's the one part of this change that is a judgment call rather than a direct translation of your instructions. 2 years → 500 sessions, 5 years → 1250 sessions. If you'd rather anchor these to literal calendar days instead (after_days=730 / 1825, which the engine already supports equally well via the same _compute_cutoff() fallback branch), that's a one-line change per rule — flagged in §Requires your approval rather than decided unilaterally.

1M's "keep 10 years, do not compress further" needed no unit decision at all — since nothing compresses from 1M, there is no rule to write. This is expressed as the simple absence of a 1M-sourced entry in the policy list, which is now a directly-tested invariant (test_1m_has_no_further_compression_rule), not an assumption.

The important shape, not just the total: six of the eight tiers are hard-bounded rolling windows that stop growing once a ticker's history exceeds their retention window — they do not grow forever.
